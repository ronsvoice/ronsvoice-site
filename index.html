Our Mission

Our mission is to bridge the gap between disability and communication through modern assistive technology, giving non-verbal individuals affordable access to tools that help them speak, express themselves, and remain connected.

Communication Devices

Providing tablets, AAC software, and adaptive communication systems to patients in need.

Family Support

Helping caregivers and families learn how to use communication technology effectively.

Accessibility

Making life-changing assistive technology more affordable and accessible to everyone.

Programs

Starting small, growing intentionally, and changing lives through meaningful technology access.

Why Ron's Voice Exists

Ron's Voice Foundation was inspired by our founder's grandfather, Ron Steele, who lived with ALS before adaptive technology as we know it existed, and spent close to a decade unable to communicate verbally with those he loved most.

Watching someone lose the ability to communicate changes the way you understand connection, dignity, and human interaction.

We believe nobody should feel trapped in silence simply because they lack access to the right technology.

Our Vision

We envision a world where no person is isolated by the inability to communicate verbally.

Through assistive technology, compassionate support, and community partnerships, we aim to restore voices and strengthen human connection.

Every donation directly helps provide communication tools to people who need them most.

Inspiration & Namesake

Ron's story serves as the foundation for our mission.

CS

Curtis Stonehocker

Founder

Created Ron's Voice Foundation to help individuals and families access communication technology and support.

My grandfather passed away long before I was even thought of, so I never had the privilege of meeting him. What I have are the stories: what an incredible man he was, how much I look like him, and how deeply he loved his family.

When I think of my grandfather, I do not think of him as a patient. I think of the man my family remembers — loving, present, and full of personality.

Growing up, I heard about the challenges he faced using early communication devices and how difficult it became for him to communicate with the people he loved most. That has always stayed with me.

I often think about how modern technology could have changed his quality of life. It bothers me that the tools he needed were either limited, unavailable, or out of reach.

To me, communication is not just words. It is how we connect, laugh, joke, make decisions, express love, and remain ourselves.

If Ron's Voice can help one patient make their own decisions, one wife hear her husband's thoughts again, or one father tell his children he loves them, then this work will be worth it.

I never got to meet my grandfather, but Ron's Voice allows me to honor him while helping others in his name.

Losing your voice does not mean losing your personality, identity, independence, or dignity. Losing your voice does not mean you cannot be heard.
